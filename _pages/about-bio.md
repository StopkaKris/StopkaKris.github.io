    <h1>Krzysztof “Kris” Stopka</h1>
    <p class="page-intro">Computational materials scientist, experimentalist, and mentor working at the intersection of advanced manufacturing, mechanics, and fatigue.</p>

      <p>I am a Lead Research Engineer in the <a href="https://engineering.purdue.edu/AAE">School of Aeronautics and Astronautics</a> at Purdue University, where I work with Dr. Michael D. Sangid in the <a href="https://engineering.purdue.edu/~msangid/">Advanced Computational Materials and Experimental Evaluation Laboratory</a>.</p>

      <p>My research lies at the interface of advanced manufacturing, materials science, crystal plasticity, computational mechanics, fatigue and fracture, scientific machine learning, and high-energy X-ray microscopy. I earned my Ph.D. from the Georgia Institute of Technology in 2021 under the advisement of Dr. David L. McDowell. My dissertation developed integrated computational materials engineering workflows for microstructure-sensitive fatigue of advanced alloys.</p>

      <p>Before graduate school, I gained industry experience at GE Aviation, Tesla, and SpaceX. These experiences continue to shape my interest in research that connects fundamental mechanics with practical materials qualification.</p>

      <p>Mentorship is central to my work, especially helping students develop confidence, independence, and a clear sense of purpose early in their academic careers. My long-term goal is to lead an academic research group that integrates mechanics-based computation, revealing experiments, and data-driven materials science.</p>
